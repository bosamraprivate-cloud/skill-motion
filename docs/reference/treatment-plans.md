## 1. Treatment plan for improving mild weakness in functional motor skills

**General goal:** To improve the range of motion of the joints and limbs, and to help the person perform daily movements with greater flexibility and ease, in the case of mild weakness in range of motion.

#### Therapeutic activities for improving range of motion

##### 1. Raising the arms overhead

- **Tools:** A light stick, or no tools.
- **How to perform:** The person stands or sits, then gradually raises their arms upward as far as they are able, and afterwards returns them to the starting position.
- **Goal:** To improve the range of motion of the shoulders.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions with comfortable, controlled movement.

##### 2. Extending and bending the elbow

- **Tools:** A light sponge ball.
- **How to perform:** The person bends their elbow to bring the ball close to their shoulder, then gradually extends their arm until it returns to the starting position.
- **Goal:** To improve the range of flexion and extension in the elbow joint.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions for each arm.

##### 3. Opening and closing the hand

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The person opens the fingers of their hand to the widest comfortable range, then closes them gently, and repeats the movement.
- **Goal:** To improve the range of motion of the fingers of the hand.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions for each hand.

##### 4. Bending and extending the wrist

- **Tools:** A light sponge ball.
- **How to perform:** The person rests their forearm steadily on a flat surface, then moves their wrist up and down slowly, while keeping the forearm still.
- **Goal:** To improve the range of motion of the wrist joint.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions for each hand.

##### 5. Extending and bending the knee

- **Tools:** A stable chair.
- **How to perform:** The person sits on the chair, then slowly extends one leg forward and returns it to the starting position, then repeats the movement with the other leg.
- **Goal:** To improve the range of motion of the knee joint.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions for each leg.

##### 6. Moving the ankle up and down

- **Tools:** A stable chair.
- **How to perform:** The person sits with their heel fixed in place, then slowly raises the front of their foot upward and lowers it downward.
- **Goal:** To improve the range of motion of the ankle joint.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions for each foot.

##### 7. Touching the shoulders alternately

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The person raises one hand to touch their opposite shoulder or the near (same-side) shoulder, according to their ability, then returns it to the starting position and repeats with the other hand.
- **Goal:** To improve shoulder movement and control of arm movement.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions, alternating between the arms.

##### 8. Side bending of the trunk

- **Tools:** A stable chair when needed.
- **How to perform:** The person stands or sits upright, then leans their trunk slightly to the right, returns to the middle, then leans to the left.
- **Goal:** To improve trunk movement in both sideways directions.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 5 repetitions for each side

### Second: Motor coordination

**General goal:** To improve the person's ability to coordinate the movements of different body parts with one another, and to control movements in a coordinated and organised manner while performing daily activities.

#### Therapeutic activities for improving motor coordination

##### 1. Passing the ball between the hands

- **Tools:** A light, medium-sized ball.
- **How to perform:** The person holds the ball with both hands, then passes it from one hand to the other in front of their body, while maintaining control of the ball.
- **Goal:** To improve coordination between the movements of the two hands and control of the movement of objects.
- **Measurement criterion:** Passing the ball 10 consecutive times without dropping it.

##### 2. Throwing and catching the ball

- **Tools:** A light sponge ball.
- **How to perform:** The person throws the ball slightly upward with both hands, then tries to catch it with both hands.
- **Goal:** To improve eye-hand coordination and the timing of movement.
- **Measurement criterion:** Catching the ball successfully in 8 attempts out of 10.

##### 3. Walking between the cones

- **Tools:** A set of small cones.
- **How to perform:** The therapist arranges the cones in a zigzag path, then the person walks between them without bumping into them.
- **Goal:** To improve coordination between the movement of the legs and control of the body's direction while walking.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing the path twice without bumping into the cones.

##### 4. Walking while moving the arms

- **Tools:** A safe space for walking.
- **How to perform:** The person walks forward while moving the right arm with the left leg, and the left arm with the right leg, in a natural manner.
- **Goal:** To improve coordination between the movements of the upper and lower limbs.
- **Measurement criterion:** Walking a distance of 5 metres while maintaining coordinated movement.

##### 5. Hitting the balloon with the hand

- **Tools:** A light balloon.
- **How to perform:** The person tries to keep the balloon in the air by hitting it with their hand, while moving according to the direction of the balloon.
- **Goal:** To improve visual-motor coordination and the speed of motor response.
- **Measurement criterion:** Keeping the balloon in the air for 20 seconds without it falling.

##### 6. Stepping up and down floor steps

- **Tools:** Low, stable training steps suited to the person's abilities.
- **How to perform:** The person steps up one step, then steps down from it, alternating between the feet, using a means of support when needed.
- **Goal:** To improve coordination between the movements of the legs and control of the body during transitions.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 5 up-and-down cycles while maintaining balance and safety.

##### 7. Imitating a sequence of movements

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The therapist performs a simple sequence, such as clapping once, then touching the shoulders, then raising the arms, and asks the person to imitate it in the same order.
- **Goal:** To improve motor coordination and the sequencing between movements.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing a sequence made up of 3 movements in the correct order in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 8. Kicking the ball toward a target

- **Tools:** A light ball and a wide target, such as a small goal.
- **How to perform:** The person stands in front of the target, then kicks the ball toward it using one foot, and repeats with the other foot according to their ability.
- **Goal:** To improve eye-foot coordination and control of the direction of movement.
- **Measurement criterion:** Directing the ball toward the target successfully in 6 attempts out of 10.

##### 9. Walking while carrying a light object

- **Tools:** A light cloth bag or a small ball.
- **How to perform:** The person walks along a straight path while carrying the object with both hands, maintaining body posture and control of movement.
- **Goal:** To improve coordination between the movement of the body and the arms while performing a motor task.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing a distance of 5 metres without dropping the object or losing balance.

##### 10. Touching the coloured targets

- **Tools:** Coloured cards fixed to the wall at a suitable height.
- **How to perform:** The therapist asks the person to touch a particular colour with one hand, then touch another colour with the other hand, while changing the order of the colours.
- **Goal:** To improve visual-motor coordination and the accuracy of directing the limbs.
- **Measurement criterion:** Touching 8 correct targets out of 10 according to the instructions

### 3. Hand skills

**General goal:** To improve control of hand and finger movements, and to develop the ability to hold objects, move them and handle them accurately during daily activities.

#### Therapeutic activities for improving hand skills

##### 1. Transferring small balls with tongs

- **Tools:** Tongs suited to the size of the hand, cotton balls or small sponge balls, and two containers.
- **How to perform:** The person holds the tongs and transfers the balls from the first container to the second, one after another.
- **Goal:** To improve finger control and visual-motor coordination.
- **Measurement criterion:** Transferring 10 balls successfully without dropping them in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 2. Picking up large beads

- **Tools:** Large, safe beads, and a thick string suited to the size of the beads.
- **How to perform:** The person picks up the beads with their fingers, then passes the string through the holes one after another.
- **Goal:** To improve eye-hand coordination and the accuracy of finger movement.
- **Measurement criterion:** Threading 5 consecutive pieces onto the string with slight assistance or without assistance.

##### 3. Shaping playdough

- **Tools:** Playdough that is safe and suitable for use by hand.
- **How to perform:** The person shapes the playdough using their fingers: pressing it, flattening it and rolling it, then making balls and simple shapes from it.
- **Goal:** To improve finger control and develop object-manipulation skills.
- **Measurement criterion:** Making 3 different shapes using hand and finger movements.

##### 4. Opening and closing clothespins

- **Tools:** Clothespins with light resistance, and the edge of a box or a piece of thick cardboard.
- **How to perform:** The person opens the clothespin using the thumb and index finger, then fixes it onto the edge and removes it again.
- **Goal:** To improve coordination between the fingers and strengthen the precision grasp.
- **Measurement criterion:** Attaching and removing 10 clothespins with control of the movement.

##### 5. Inserting coins or discs into a slot

- **Tools:** A box with a relatively wide slot, and large discs suited to the size of the hand.
- **How to perform:** The person picks up each disc with their fingertips, then directs it to the slot and inserts it.
- **Goal:** To improve the precision grasp and control of directing the fingers.
- **Measurement criterion:** Inserting 8 discs out of 10 without physical assistance.

##### 6. Assembling blocks

- **Tools:** Large or medium-sized building blocks.
- **How to perform:** The person holds the blocks and fits them on top of one another or next to one another to form a simple model.
- **Goal:** To improve control of the hands and the accuracy of placing objects.
- **Measurement criterion:** Building a tower of 6 blocks without it falling.

##### 7. Tracing lines with a pen

- **Tools:** A sheet of paper and a thick, easy-to-hold pen.
- **How to perform:** The person traces pre-drawn straight lines, zigzag lines and circles, while trying to stay inside the path.
- **Goal:** To improve pen control and coordination of finger and wrist movements.
- **Measurement criterion:** Tracing 4 paths while staying within the boundaries for most of the path.

##### 8. Using the pincer grasp with the thumb and index finger

- **Tools:** Small sponge pieces or relatively large objects that are easy to pick up.
- **How to perform:** The person picks up each piece using the thumb and index finger, then moves it to a specified place.
- **Goal:** To improve the precision grasp and finger control.
- **Measurement criterion:** Transferring 10 pieces while using the fingers appropriately.

##### 9. Twisting lids

- **Tools:** Clean plastic containers with large, easy-to-open lids.
- **How to perform:** The person holds the container with one hand and uses the other hand to unscrew the lid and screw it back on.
- **Goal:** To improve finger control and coordination between the two hands.
- **Measurement criterion:** Opening and closing the lid 5 times with control of the movement.

##### 10. Moving sheets of paper using the fingers

- **Tools:** Light sheets of paper or large cards.
- **How to perform:** The person picks up the cards one after another using their fingertips, then turns them over or places them in another pile.
- **Goal:** To improve the accuracy of finger movement and control of light objects.
- **Measurement criterion:** Moving 10 cards without tearing them or dropping them repeatedly

### 4. Midline crossing

**General goal:** To improve the person's ability to move their hand or arm across the imaginary midline of the body to reach objects located on the opposite side, which helps improve motor coordination and the performance of daily activities.

The midline is an imaginary line that divides the body into two halves: right and left.

#### Therapeutic activities for improving midline crossing

##### 1. Touching the coloured balls

- **Tools:** Coloured balls, and surfaces or markers to define where they are placed.
- **How to perform:** The person sits at the table, and the balls are placed on both sides of them. They are asked to use their right hand to pick up a ball from the left side and place it in a container on the right side, then to use their left hand to reach to the right side and move the ball to the left.
- **Goal:** To improve the hand's ability to cross the midline while reaching for objects.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing 8 correct attempts out of 10 using the specified hand.

##### 2. Drawing on the board

- **Tools:** A board or a large sheet of paper, and suitable pens.
- **How to perform:** The person draws circles or long horizontal lines that start from one side of the sheet and extend to the other side, using one hand.
- **Goal:** To improve arm movement across the midline while performing drawing tasks.
- **Measurement criterion:** Drawing 5 continuous lines that cross the midline with appropriate control of the movement.

##### 3. Picking up rings and placing them on the cones

- **Tools:** Plastic rings and small cones.
- **How to perform:** The rings are placed on one side of the body, and the cones on the opposite side. The person picks up the rings with one hand and reaches their arm across the midline to place them on the cones.
- **Goal:** To improve reaching across the midline and visual-motor coordination.
- **Measurement criterion:** Transferring 8 rings out of 10 using the target hand.

##### 4. Passing the ball around the body

- **Tools:** A light, medium-sized ball.
- **How to perform:** The person holds the ball with both hands in front of their body, then transfers it to one hand and passes it in front of the body to the other side, repeating the movement in both directions.
- **Goal:** To improve coordination between the arms and control of the ball's movement across the middle of the body.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing 10 consecutive passes with control of the ball.

##### 5. Touching opposite body parts

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The person is asked to use their right hand to touch their left knee, then to use their left hand to touch their right knee. This can be progressed to touching the opposite shoulder if that is comfortable.
- **Goal:** To improve midline crossing and coordination between the two sides of the body.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 8 correct movements out of 10.

##### 6. Picking up cards from the opposite side

- **Tools:** Coloured cards and a container.
- **How to perform:** The cards are placed on the left side of the table, and the person is asked to use their right hand to pick them up and place them in a container on the right side, then to reverse the direction using the other hand.
- **Goal:** To improve midline crossing during goal-directed reaching.
- **Measurement criterion:** Transferring 8 cards out of 10 with the target hand.

##### 7. Cleaning the board with wide movements

- **Tools:** A small board and a suitable eraser.
- **How to perform:** The person holds the eraser with one hand, then wipes the board with wide horizontal movements from right to left, then from left to right, while keeping the arm movement crossing the middle of the body.
- **Goal:** To improve midline crossing and control of arm movement.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 5 continuous horizontal movements in each direction.

##### 8. Placing stickers on a panel

- **Tools:** Large stickers and a panel fixed in front of the person.
- **How to perform:** The stickers are placed on one side, and the person is asked to pick them up with one hand and stick them at specified points on the opposite side of the panel, then to switch between the hands.
- **Goal:** To improve reaching across the midline and visual-motor coordination.
- **Measurement criterion:** Placing 8 stickers out of 10 in the specified places.

##### 9. Hitting the balloon across the body

- **Tools:** A light balloon.
- **How to perform:** The person stands or sits in a stable position, then tries to touch the balloon with their right hand when it moves to the left side of the body, and with their left hand when it moves to the right side.
- **Goal:** To improve midline crossing and eye-hand coordination.
- **Measurement criterion:** Touching the balloon successfully in 8 attempts out of 10.

##### 10. Transferring blocks across the midline

- **Tools:** Blocks and two containers.
- **How to perform:** The first container is placed on the left side and the second on the right side. The person transfers the blocks from one container to the other using one hand, so that the hand crosses the midline during the transfer, then repeats the activity with the other hand in the opposite direction.
- **Goal:** To improve midline crossing while performing a functional task that requires accuracy.
- **Measurement criterion:** Transferring 10 blocks, with at least 8 attempts completed correctly

### 5. Motor planning

**General goal:** To improve the person's ability to plan, organise and carry out movements in the appropriate order to achieve a specific motor goal.

#### Therapeutic activities for improving motor planning

##### 1. Motor course

- **Tools:** Cones, hoops, and a low sponge barrier.
- **How to perform:** The person is asked to walk between the cones, then step over the barrier, then jump inside the hoop, according to a specified order.
- **Goal:** To improve the ability to plan and organise sequential movements.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing the course in the correct order in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 2. Imitating body postures

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The therapist demonstrates a simple motor posture, such as raising the arms or standing on one foot, and asks the person to imitate it.
- **Goal:** To improve the ability to plan the movements needed to reach a specific posture.
- **Measurement criterion:** Imitating 4 correct postures out of 5.

##### 3. Performing a series of movements

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The therapist asks the person to perform a series of three movements, such as clapping, then touching the knees, then raising the arms overhead.
- **Goal:** To improve the organisation of movements and carrying them out in the required sequence.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing the sequence in the correct order in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 4. Imitating a motor pattern

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The therapist performs a motor pattern, such as touching the right shoulder, then the left knee, then clapping, and asks the person to repeat it in the same order.
- **Goal:** To improve the planning of multiple movements and the organisation of the transition between them.
- **Measurement criterion:** Repeating the motor pattern correctly in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 5. Reaching a target while getting past an obstacle

- **Tools:** A ball, cones, and a box.
- **How to perform:** The ball is placed in a specified place, and the person is asked to reach it by going around the cones, then to pick it up and place it inside the box.
- **Goal:** To improve planning of the motor path and organisation of the steps for reaching the target.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing the task in the required order in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 6. Building a model with blocks

- **Tools:** Coloured blocks.
- **How to perform:** The therapist shows a simple model made up of several blocks, then asks the person to rebuild it in the same order and shape.
- **Goal:** To improve the motor planning associated with organising and sequencing hand movements.
- **Measurement criterion:** Rebuilding the model correctly in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 7. Throwing the ball toward multiple targets

- **Tools:** Light balls and baskets or designated targets.
- **How to perform:** The therapist sets different locations for the targets, and asks the person to choose the appropriate ball, determine the direction of the movement, then throw it toward the required target.
- **Goal:** To improve planning of the movement and determining its direction in a way that suits the target.
- **Measurement criterion:** Hitting the specified target in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 8. Imitating animal movements

- **Tools:** A safe space for movement.
- **How to perform:** The person is asked to imitate simple movements, such as walking like a bear, hopping like a rabbit, or walking sideways, moving from one movement to another according to a specified order.
- **Goal:** To improve the planning of new movements and the organisation of the transition between them.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 3 motor patterns in the correct order in 4 attempts out of 5.

##### 9. Carrying out a task using multiple tools

- **Tools:** A ball, a hoop, and a box.
- **How to perform:** The person is asked to pick up the ball, then pass through the hoop, then place the ball in the box, with the steps arranged in advance.
- **Goal:** To improve purposeful motor planning and organisation of the steps of the task.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing the task in the correct order without exceeding the number of assists specified in advance.

##### 10. Learning a new movement

- **Tools:** Simple tools according to the required movement.
- **How to perform:** The therapist explains a new movement made up of several steps, then demonstrates it in front of the person and lets them try it, while gradually reducing assistance.
- **Goal:** To improve the ability to acquire new movements and to plan and carry them out independently.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing the new movement correctly in 4 attempts out of 5

## 2. Treatment plan for improving moderate weakness in functional motor skills

### First: Motor planning

**General goal:** Improve the child's ability to plan, organize, and sequence different movements, and to understand the steps needed to carry out motor tasks correctly and in an organized manner.

#### Therapeutic activities for improving motor planning

##### 1. Imitating a sequence of movements

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The therapist performs a set of consecutive movements, such as raising the arms, then clapping, then touching the head, and asks the child to imitate them in the same order.
- **Goal:** Improve the child's ability to plan movements, organize them, and carry them out in sequence.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing a sequence of 3 movements in the correct order in 4 out of 5 attempts.

##### 2. Completing an obstacle course

- **Tools:** Small cones and low foam obstacles.
- **How to perform:** The therapist sets up a course that includes walking between the cones, then stepping over a small obstacle, then reaching the end point, and asks the child to complete the course in the specified order.
- **Goal:** Improve motor planning and the organization of the transition from one movement to another.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing the whole course correctly in 3 out of 4 attempts.

##### 3. Carrying out consecutive motor commands

- **Tools:** A set of simple toys.
- **How to perform:** The therapist gives the child consecutive instructions, such as standing up, then picking up the ball, then placing it inside the box, and asks them to carry them out in the same order.
- **Goal:** Improve the ability to plan and organize movements according to instructions.
- **Measurement criterion:** Carrying out 3 consecutive motor commands correctly in 4 out of 5 attempts.

##### 4. Building a tower according to a model

- **Tools:** Coloured cubes of different sizes.
- **How to perform:** The therapist shows a model of a tower made of several cubes, and asks the child to observe their arrangement and then build a similar tower using the available cubes.
- **Goal:** Improve visual motor planning and the organization of the steps for carrying out the activity.
- **Measurement criterion:** Building a tower of 5 cubes according to the correct model in 3 out of 4 attempts.

##### 5. Jumping inside the hoops

- **Tools:** A set of coloured hoops.
- **How to perform:** The therapist arranges the hoops on the floor in different patterns, and asks the child to jump inside them in a particular order, such as red, then blue, then yellow.
- **Goal:** Improve the ability to plan a movement and determine its direction and sequence.
- **Measurement criterion:** Jumping inside 5 hoops in the required order in 4 out of 5 attempts.

##### 6. Imitating animal movements

- **Tools:** Picture cards of different animals.
- **How to perform:** The therapist shows a card of a particular animal and asks the child to imitate the way it moves, such as jumping like a rabbit, walking like a bear, or crawling like a snake.
- **Goal:** Improve the ability to create and organize movements according to a specific motor model.
- **Measurement criterion:** Imitating 4 different animal movements correctly in 4 out of 5 attempts.

##### 7. Transferring objects in a specified order

- **Tools:** Coloured balls and two baskets.
- **How to perform:** The therapist places the balls in one basket, and asks the child to transfer them to the other basket in a particular order of colours, while following the instructions.
- **Goal:** Improve motor planning and the organization of the steps for performing the task.
- **Measurement criterion:** Transferring 8 balls in the required order in 4 out of 5 attempts.

##### 8. Completing a varied motor course

- **Tools:** A play tunnel, hoops, and cones.
- **How to perform:** The therapist asks the child to crawl through the tunnel, then jump inside a hoop, then walk between the cones until reaching the end point.
- **Goal:** Improve the ability to plan and organize a set of different movements.
- **Measurement criterion:** Completing the 3-stage course in the correct order in 3 out of 4 attempts.

##### 9. Imitating body positions

- **Tools:** Cards showing different body positions.
- **How to perform:** The therapist shows a card containing a particular position, such as standing on one foot or stretching out the arms, and asks the child to perform the same position.
- **Goal:** Improve the ability to understand motor positions and plan the appropriate body response.
- **Measurement criterion:** Imitating 4 different positions correctly in 4 out of 5 attempts.

##### 10. Ordering the steps of a motor task

- **Tools:** Simple items such as an empty cup, a small bottle, and a box.
- **How to perform:** The therapist asks the child to carry out a task made up of several steps, such as picking up the bottle, then pouring a suitable amount of water into the cup, then putting the bottle back in its place.
- **Goal:** Improve motor planning and the organization and sequencing of the steps needed to complete functional tasks.
- **Measurement criterion:** Carrying out 3 consecutive steps in the correct order in 4 out of 5 attempts.

### Second: Range of motion

**General goal:** Improve the child's ability to move the different body parts and joints in various directions, and increase the efficiency of movement while performing daily activities.

#### Therapeutic activities for improving range of motion

##### 1. Raising the arms upward

- **Tools:** A light stick or a coloured toy.
- **How to perform:** The child holds the toy with both hands and gradually raises their arms upward, then returns them to the normal position.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the shoulder joint and increase the ability to raise the arms.
- **Measurement criterion:** Raising the arms upward and lowering them 10 consecutive times without pain.

##### 2. Opening and closing the arms

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The child stands and opens their arms out to the sides, then brings them together in front of the chest, repeatedly.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the shoulder joint and control of arm movement.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions of opening and closing the arms correctly.

##### 3. Extending and bending the elbow

- **Tools:** A small sponge ball.
- **How to perform:** The child holds the ball in their hand, then bends the elbow to bring the ball close to the shoulder, and after that extends the arm again.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the elbow joint.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions of bending and extending for each arm correctly.

##### 4. Rotating the wrists

- **Tools:** A small light ball.
- **How to perform:** The child holds the ball or places their hands in front of them, then moves their wrists in circular movements, clockwise and counter-clockwise.
- **Goal:** Improve the movement of the wrist joint and increase its motor flexibility.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 5 circular rotations in each direction for each hand.

##### 5. Opening and closing the fingers

- **Tools:** No tools needed.
- **How to perform:** The therapist asks the child to open the fingers of both hands fully, then bring them together and close them gradually, repeatedly.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the fingers and control of their movements.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions of opening and closing the fingers correctly.

##### 6. Raising the knees alternately

- **Tools:** A safe space suitable for movement.
- **How to perform:** The child stands upright, then raises the right knee upward and returns it to the floor, and after that raises the left knee.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the hip joint and the knee.
- **Measurement criterion:** Raising each knee 10 times alternately while maintaining balance.

##### 7. Extending and bending the knees while sitting

- **Tools:** A stable chair suited to the child's height.
- **How to perform:** The child sits on the chair and extends one leg forward, then bends it, and repeats the movement with the other leg.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion in the knee joint and control of leg movement.
- **Measurement criterion:** Performing 10 repetitions of extending and bending for each leg correctly.

##### 8. Touching targets in different directions

- **Tools:** Coloured cards or small toys.
- **How to perform:** The therapist fixes the targets in different places in front of the child and on both sides, and asks them to extend their arm to reach them in the different directions.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the shoulder and arm and the ability to reach in multiple directions.
- **Measurement criterion:** Reaching 8 out of 10 targets without pain or obvious compensatory movement.

##### 9. Extending the legs while sitting

- **Tools:** A stable chair and a light ball.
- **How to perform:** The child sits on the chair and places the ball in front of their feet, then extends their legs forward, trying to touch the ball with their feet alternately.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the hip and knee joints and control of the movement of the lower limbs.
- **Measurement criterion:** Touching the ball with each foot 5 consecutive times correctly.

##### 10. Bending down to pick up objects

- **Tools:** Cubes or light toys placed on the floor.
- **How to perform:** The therapist asks the child to bend down gradually to pick up the toys placed in front of them, then return to the standing position.
- **Goal:** Improve the range of motion of the trunk and the hip and knee joints while performing a functional task.
- **Measurement criterion:** Picking up 8 out of 10 toys while returning to the standing position safely.
